\subsubsection*{Project Overview — Two Approaches for Time-Series Classification}

This project addresses \textbf{Human Activity Recognition (HAR)} using two complementary strategies
inspired by \textit{Chapter 6 — Classification de séries temporelles}.

Each strategy provides a distinct representation and analytical framework:
one focuses on temporal similarity (\textit{Direct Approach}),
the other on compact statistical descriptors (\textit{Feature-Based Approach}).

\vspace{0.5cm}
\begin{center}
\begin{tabular}{ p{0.18\textwidth} p{0.35\textwidth} p{0.17\textwidth} p{0.25\textwidth} } 
\toprule
\textbf{Approach} & \textbf{Principle} & \textbf{Data Format} & \textbf{Main Algorithms} \\ 
\midrule

\textbf{1. Direct (Distance-Based)} &
Works directly on raw temporal sequences (128×9), comparing complete signal windows using time-aware distance metrics. &
Tensor $(347 \times 128 \times 9)$ &
Euclidean • Correlation • DTW $\rightarrow$ K-Means (DTW-DBA), PAM, CAH \\

\addlinespace[4pt]

\textbf{2. Feature-Based (Transformation)} &
Transforms each temporal window into a fixed-length vector of statistical \& spectral descriptors
(mean, std, skewness, kurtosis, energy, AR coefficients, etc.). &
Matrix $(347 \times p)$ &
K-Means • GMM • SOM • Hierarchical (Ward) \\

\bottomrule
\end{tabular}
\end{center}

\vspace{0.6cm}
Both representations will be thoroughly explored during the \textbf{Exploratory Data Analysis (EDA)} phase.
The goal is to study the intrinsic structure and separability of the data,
ensuring robust preparation for the clustering and evaluation stages.

\vspace{0.5cm}
\begin{center}
\rule{0.75\textwidth}{0.4pt}\\
\textit{Together, these two approaches provide a unified and comprehensive view of human activity dynamics — 
combining temporal precision and statistical interpretability.}
\end{center}

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Data Loading \& Structuring}
Two complementary representations were prepared, following the methodology of
\textit{Chapter 6 — Classification de séries temporelles}:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=1.2cm, label=--, itemsep=2pt]
    \item $X$ — 3D tensor $(347, 128, 9)$ representing raw time-series (Direct Approach).
    \item $Z$ — 2D feature matrix $(347, p)$ summarizing each window statistically (Feature-Based).
    \item $y$ — label vector $(347,)$ containing true activity classes (1–6).
\end{itemize}

Each of the 347 samples corresponds to a 2.56-second window (128 steps × 9 sensor variables: accelerations and velocities).  
The file \texttt{lab.txt} contains the reference labels used for external evaluation.

\chapter{II. Clustering Analysis and Visualization}

This section presents the comparative study of clustering algorithms applied to both representations:
the \textbf{Feature-Based} and the \textbf{Time-Series (Direct)} approaches.
It integrates visualization, quantitative evaluation, and interpretation of results from multiple algorithms
(K-Means, CAH, GMM, DBSCAN, SOM, DTW-KMeans, etc.).

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Imports and Data Loading}
All required libraries (Scikit-learn, Matplotlib, Pandas, Tslearn, etc.) were imported, and the pre-processed datasets
(\texttt{features.npy}, \texttt{labels.npy}, and \texttt{tensor\_X.npy}) were loaded from the EDA phase.
Both representations share synchronized labels (\( y \in \mathbb{R}^{347} \)) ensuring a fair comparison.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Load Features and Labels}
The normalized feature matrix \( Z \in \mathbb{R}^{347 \times 54} \) and the time-series tensor
\( X \in \mathbb{R}^{347 \times 128 \times 9} \) were loaded and verified.
These datasets form the basis for the two methodological families of clustering.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Run All Clustering Methods}
Dimensionality reduction (PCA, UMAP, t-SNE) was applied before clustering to reduce redundancy,
enhance distance consistency, and reveal intrinsic geometry.

\subsubsection*{Impact of Dimensionality Reduction on Cluster Detection}
Without dimensionality reduction, most partition-based methods (KMeans, GMM, CAH, Spectral, Birch)
consistently produced 6 clusters as expected.
However, adaptive and density-based algorithms exhibited variable behavior:
\begin{itemize}[label=--, leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{AffinityPropagation:} over-segmented the space (26 clusters).
    \item \textbf{DBSCAN:} identified one dense region only.
    \item \textbf{OPTICS} and \textbf{HDBSCAN:} found 5 and 3 clusters respectively.
    \item \textbf{SOM:} activated 68 neurons, reflecting fine local granularity.
\end{itemize}

After \textbf{PCA}, feature redundancy decreased and cluster compactness improved:
Affinity dropped to 18 clusters, while HDBSCAN and OPTICS stabilized near 4–5 clusters.

Using \textbf{UMAP}, which preserves local structure, density-based models captured
more meaningful neighborhood separations (DBSCAN → 7, HDBSCAN → 5 clusters).
Linear reduction (PCA) enhances homogeneity, while nonlinear reduction (UMAP) improves local separability,
making clusters more consistent with the intrinsic manifold.

% -------------------------------------------------------------
\section{Quantitative Evaluation (All Methods)}
Across approximately 49 clustering configurations — combining both \textbf{Feature-Based} and \textbf{Time-Series} approaches 
with various dimensionality reductions (\textit{None}, \textit{PCA}, \textit{UMAP}, and \textit{t-SNE}) — the following quantitative trends were observed:

\begin{itemize}[label=--, leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item \textbf{Highest internal cohesion:} \textbf{HDBSCAN + PCA} achieved the best overall Silhouette score ($0.379$), 
    indicating compact and well-separated clusters after linear reduction.
    
    \item \textbf{Strongest overall compactness:} \textbf{HDBSCAN (no reduction)} recorded the maximum Silhouette value ($0.462$),
    reflecting that the algorithm natively adapts well to the intrinsic structure of the feature space without preprocessing.
    
    \item \textbf{Best external alignment:} \textbf{KMeans (t-SNE projection)} yielded an outstanding 
    Adjusted Rand Index ($ARI = 0.98$) and Normalized Mutual Information ($NMI = 0.978$),
    confirming excellent agreement between predicted clusters and true activity labels.
    
    \item \textbf{Feature-Based models:} Classical algorithms such as \textbf{GMM}, \textbf{CAH-Ward}, and \textbf{Birch}
    maintained solid $ARI$ values (0.59–0.81) with consistent Calinski–Harabasz indices ($>90{,}000$),
    indicating stable geometric cohesion and moderate cluster overlap.
    
    \item \textbf{Dimensionality reduction effects:}
    \begin{itemize}[label=--, leftmargin=1.5cm]
        \item \textbf{PCA} improved linear separability, stabilizing density-based methods like HDBSCAN and Birch.
        \item \textbf{UMAP} captured local nonlinear patterns ($NMI \approx 0.85$), reinforcing interpretability.
        \item \textbf{t-SNE} revealed clearer substructures with very high label fidelity but weaker Silhouette due to crowding effects.
    \end{itemize}

    \item \textbf{Time-Series clustering:} 
    Models such as \textbf{DTW-KMeans + TimeSeriesData} and \textbf{KShape + TimeSeriesData} displayed strong external consistency 
    ($ARI \approx 0.93$–$0.94$, $NMI \approx 0.95$), confirming their ability to capture phase shifts and temporal dynamics 
    in the raw signal representation.
\end{itemize}

\vspace{0.2cm}
\noindent\textbf{Summary of observations:}
\begin{itemize}[label=--, leftmargin=1.2cm, itemsep=2pt]
    \item The \textbf{Feature-Based} framework (especially \textbf{HDBSCAN + PCA}) offered robust and interpretable partitions, 
    emphasizing statistical separability across activities.
    \item The \textbf{Time-Series} approach (notably \textbf{DTW-KMeans + TimeSeriesData}) 
    excelled at preserving dynamic continuity and temporal coherence between similar motions.
\end{itemize}
